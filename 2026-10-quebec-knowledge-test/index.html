After you pass

  1. Book the road test on SAAQclic. It costs $33.75.
  2. Print and sign the consent form for non-agreement licence holders. Bring it to the road test.
  3. Bring your own roadworthy car with its registration and a paper copy of the insurance. You can’t bring passengers or pets.
Bring someone with a licence to the road test. If you fail it, you can no longer drive on your Australian licence. You get a Québec learner’s licence instead: you must be accompanied, can’t drive midnight–5 a.m. and must have zero alcohol. That person can then drive you home.

1

Highway Safety Code
  • Right turn on red: banned everywhere on the Island of Montréal. Elsewhere it’s allowed unless a sign forbids it. Come to a full stop first, as at a stop sign, then yield.
  • Traffic lights out: treat the intersection as an all-way stop.
  • All-way stop: the first vehicle to stop goes first. If two stop at the same time, yield to the vehicle on your right. Pedestrians already crossing have priority.
  • Left turns: if the oncoming car is also turning left, turn in front of it, not behind it.
  • School bus with flashing red lights or stop arm: stop more than 5 m away, from either direction, on any number of lanes. The only exception is oncoming traffic separated by a median or other divider. Four alternating yellow lights or hazards mean get ready to stop.
  • City bus signalling to pull out where the limit is under 70 km/h: yield to it.
  • Emergency vehicle with siren or lights: clear the lane and stop if necessary. Don’t follow it afterwards. Slowing down in your own lane is not enough.
  • Move-over law: for a stopped emergency vehicle, tow truck or patrol vehicle with flashing lights or arrow, slow down and move over a lane if safe. If you can’t move over, stop. Penalty: $200–300 and 4 points.
  • Default speed limits: 50 km/h in towns, 90 on secondary roads, 100 on most highways. Montréal school zones are usually posted at 30.
  • Excessive speeding means 40+ over in a zone of 60 or less, 50+ over in a zone above 60 up to 90, or 60+ over in a zone of 100 or more. Penalty: immediate 7-day licence suspension, then double the points and the fine.
  • Fines double for speeding in road work zones and in school zones during the school year.
  • Alcohol: 0.08 BAC is a Criminal Code offence. Zero alcohol applies to drivers under 22 and all learner and probationary licence holders. Zero drugs applies to everyone. Nobody may smoke cannabis in a vehicle.
  • Hand-held devices: holding a phone while driving costs $300–600 and 5 points. Stopped at a red light or in traffic still counts as driving. Hands-free is legal.
  • Demerit point limits: 15 points at age 25 or older, 12 at 23–24, 8 under 23, and 4 for learner or probationary licences. Points stay on your record for 2 years. Driving while revoked means a 30-day vehicle seizure.
  • Winter tires: four, in good condition, from December 1 to March 15.
  • Always carry your licence, the vehicle registration and proof of insurance (third-party liability of at least $50,000).
  • Cyclists and pedestrians: when passing, leave 1 m in zones of 50 km/h or less and 1.5 m in faster zones. Stop for a pedestrian who steps onto a crosswalk or shows they want to cross. Don’t stop on a bike path or within 5 m of one.
  • Roundabouts: enter to the right and go counter-clockwise. Yield to vehicles, cyclists and pedestrians already in the roundabout.
  • Seat belts are required in every seat. The driver is responsible for passengers under 16. Passengers 16 and over pay their own fine.
  • Child seats until 145 cm tall or 9 years old. Children 12 and under should ride in the back.
  • Horn: only to warn of danger or signal your presence. Honking in anger can cost $100 or more.
  • Lanes: never cross two lanes in one move. The centre lane of a 3- or 5-lane two-way road is for left turns only, never for passing.
  • Railway crossing with flashing lights: stop at least 5 m from the nearest rail. Buses, minibuses and trucks carrying hazardous materials always stop, so expect them to.
  • Shared street: 20 km/h and pedestrians have priority. Bike street: 30 km/h and cyclists can use the whole lane.

Road signs, signals & markings
  • Solid red means stop before the stop line or crosswalk. Lights sit on the far side of the intersection, so don’t stop under them.
  • Yellow (amber) means stop, unless you’re so close you can’t stop safely.
  • Flashing green or a green arrow means a protected left turn. Oncoming traffic has a red.
  • Flashing red means stop, yield, then go, like a stop sign. Flashing yellow means slow down and yield to anyone already in the intersection.
  • Red arrow means no movement in that direction. A yellow arrow works like a yellow light for that direction.
  • Pedestrian lights: a white walking figure means pedestrians may cross. An orange hand means don’t start crossing; when it flashes, people already crossing should hurry.
  • Yellow lines separate traffic going in opposite directions. White lines separate lanes going the same way and also mark stop lines and crosswalks.
  • Broken line means you may cross it to pass when safe. Solid line means don’t cross. With a solid and a broken line side by side, only the side with the broken line may pass.
  • White diamond on a sign or the pavement marks a reserved lane, e.g. for buses and taxis, with hours shown on the sign.
  • Orange signs mark road work. Their speed limits are mandatory, and speeding fines double.
  • Flagperson: an “Arrêt” sign means stop and wait. A “Lentement” sign means slow down and go carefully.
  • You may cross a solid line to pass a slow vehicle with an orange triangle, or farm machinery, when it’s safe to do so.

Driving a passenger vehicle (your PDF)

Ch 1–2 · Ready to drive, scanning

  • The scanning method is Observe–Evaluate–Act.
  • Check your mirrors every 10–12 s, and before slowing, stopping, turning or changing lanes.
  • You lose 20° of field of vision for every 15 km/h of speed.
  • The right mirror makes vehicles look farther away than they are.
  • To check a blind spot, glance quickly over your shoulder without moving your body. Keep your speed steady.
  • Keep at least 25 cm between your sternum and the wheel’s airbag. The middle of the headrest should be at eye level or the top of your ears, no more than 7 cm from your head.
  • Hold the wheel at 9 and 3. Use hand-over-hand for more than half a turn.
  • Red warning lights mean a problem: stop and see a mechanic. Orange or yellow lights are reminders. Green shows direction.

Ch 3 · Controlling the vehicle

  • Double your speed and kinetic energy goes up 4×. Triple it and energy goes up 9×.
  • Two cars meeting head-on at 50 km/h hit like one car at 100 km/h into a wall. A 100 km/h crash is like falling 12 storeys.
  • If a crash can’t be avoided, slow as much as possible. Aim for something that gives, like a hedge or snowbank, and hit at an angle rather than head-on.
  • Parking downhill: turn the wheels toward the curb. Uphill: turn them toward the street. Park within 30 cm of the curb.
  • Come to a full stop before shifting N→R or R→P.
  • Merging onto an expressway: signal left before the end of the ramp, use the whole acceleration lane and never stop in it. Exiting: slow down only once you’re in the deceleration lane.
  • After passing, return to your lane once the passed car is fully visible in your inside mirror. When being passed, keep your speed and stay in the centre of your lane.

Ch 4 · Sharing the road

  • Stopped behind a car, you should see its rear tires, about 5 m back.
  • On snow or ice, leave a gap of at least 8–10 s.
  • Total stopping distance = perception + reaction + braking.
  • Motorcycles stop in a shorter distance, so leave them more room. They’re entitled to the whole lane.
  • Slow-moving vehicles travel at 40 km/h or less and carry an orange triangle with a red border.
  • Most pedestrian crashes happen between 3 and 7 p.m. in zones of 50 km/h or less.
  • Dutch reach: open the door with your far hand so you turn and see cyclists.
  • Use low beams in tunnels. Turn on your wipers before passing a truck on a wet road.

Ch 5 · Strategies & emergencies

  • Dim your high beams within 150 m of a vehicle, whether oncoming or ahead of you.
  • In fog, use low beams. If it’s very thick, pull off, turn on hazards and turn off headlights.
  • Rain is most slippery in its first few minutes. Avoid cruise control on wet, snowy or winding roads.
  • ABS: press hard and hold, and don’t pump. Without ABS, brake just short of locking the wheels.
  • In a skid, steer where you want to go and release the brake. If you hydroplane, ease off the accelerator gradually.
  • If a tire blows, grip the wheel firmly and hold your speed. Don’t brake until you have control.
  • With two wheels on the shoulder, ease off the gas without braking, then steer gently back.
  • Stuck accelerator: shift to N, brake and pull over. Brake failure: pump the pedal, then apply the parking brake gradually.
  • Stuck in a snowstorm: run the engine 10 minutes per hour with the exhaust clear and a window cracked.
  • Car fire: stay 30 m away. Live wires on the car: stay inside, or jump clear if you must get out.
  • A temporary spare tire is limited to 80 km/h. Animals are most active in May, June, October and November, at dawn and dusk.

Ch 6 · Preventive attitude

  • The three kinds of distraction are visual, physical and intellectual. Hands-free calls are not safer, because the conversation itself distracts you.
  • Take a break every 2 h and drive no more than 4 h at a time. Microsleeps last 3–4 s.
  • Alcohol is a depressant. Coffee, food and cold showers don’t lower your BAC.
  • If someone pressures you, pull over and let them pass. Don’t react to aggressive drivers.

Accidents

  • Protect the scene, call for help, then help the injured.
  • Don’t give an injured person anything to drink. Don’t remove a motorcyclist’s helmet. Don’t move anyone unless they’re in danger.
  • Aim an extinguisher at the base of the flames. Never use water on a fuel fire.
  • If you’re in an accident and don’t offer help, that’s an offence.
